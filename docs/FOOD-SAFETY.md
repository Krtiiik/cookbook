# Food-safety sources

Source order for every food-safety number on the site: first a Czech or EU authority (SZÚ, bezpecnostpotravin.cz (the Ministerstvo zemědělství portal), SZPI, Ministerstvo zdravotnictví and its regional public health offices (KHS), EFSA, EU regulations). Next, another European authority (BfR in Germany, FSA/NHS in the UK). USDA FSIS, FoodKeeper or NCHFP only when no Czech or European source gives a number. When sources differ, the site uses the stricter number. In the Czech text, name the source short: "podle SZÚ", "podle bezpecnostpotravin.cz", "podle KHS", "podle BfR", "podle britského úřadu FSA", "podle USDA FSIS". Numbers below were read from the pages on 2026-11-24.

Note: decree 137/2004 Sb. used to set 75 °C for 5 minutes for cooking, 70 °C for reheating and 5 days for chilled dishes (§§ 21, 31, 32). These sections are repealed since 1 Jan 2007 (current text: https://www.zakonyprolidi.cz/cs/2004-137). Do not cite them. The copy on bezpecnostpotravin.cz is the old 2004 text.

## 5. Raw beef bones

Freeze on the day you buy them, or keep 1–2 days in the fridge (as topic 4). Safe at −18 °C almost without limit, "podle bezpecnostpotravin.cz" (https://bezpecnostpotravin.cz/jak-zachazet-s-potravinami/). No source has a number for bones; the quality limit 3–4 months stays "podle USDA FSIS".

## 6. Butter

**No Czech/EU home number. USDA FoodKeeper stays** (fridge 1–2 months, freezer 6–9 months, quality). Czech law has only labelling rules: "čerstvé máslo" within 20 days of production; "stolní máslo" stored at ≤−18 °C for at most 24 months (vyhláška 397/2016 Sb., per bezpecnostpotravin.cz, https://bezpecnostpotravin.cz/termin/oznacovani-tuku/). These are not home storage advice; do not use them as such.

## 19. Mould on fruit, jam and povidla; patulin

Extends topic 8. BfR names Kompott, Fruchtsaft, Konfitüre, Gelee and Obst as especially prone; mycotoxins are heat- and acid-stable, so throw the whole fruit or jar away and never cook kompot or mošt from rotten apples, "podle BfR" (topic 8 URL) and EU Recommendation 2003/598/EC (https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reco/2003/598/oj/eng). Only a firm bruise may be cut out of an apple. Birch-pollen allergy: cooked apple is usually tolerated, not by everyone, "podle britské NHS" (https://guysandstthomas.nhs.uk/health-information/pollen-food-syndrome). Verbraucherzentrale allows trimming small spots; the site keeps the stricter rule. Opened jam goes in the fridge (no day count exists), reduced-sugar 2:1 and 3:1 jam especially. Cooked povidla follow topic 1.

## 21. Burns (karamel)

Cool under running cold, not iced, water for at least 20 minutes and until the pain eases, "podle britské NHS" (https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/burns-and-scalds/treatment/); Český červený kříž gives at least 10 minutes (via VZP: https://www.vzp.cz/o-nas/aktuality/vazne-popaleniny-hrozi-i-u-bezneho-grilovani-casteji-skonci-v-nemocnici-muzi-nez-zeny; the ČČK PDF was unread). No ice; do not tear off what sticks; for large burns, the face, the palms or small children call 155. Solid sugar needs no best-before date (nařízení EU 1169/2011, příloha X).

## 25. Dried fruit and kernels

Prunes may carry sulphites up to 2 000 mg/kg (nařízení ES 1333/2008, příloha II, 04.2.1); above 10 mg/kg they must be labelled as an allergen (nařízení EU 1169/2011, příloha II, bod 12). Plum kernels: no Czech or EU source (BfR and EFSA cover apricot kernels only); the plum page cites the US Poison Control center for "do not crush or chew the kernel". Bottled lemon juice is often sulphited (MENDELU found 10 of 22 samples: https://doi.mendelu.cz/pdfs/doi/9900/06/9900.pdf); wine, wine and balsamic vinegar, dijonská and imported mustard (E224) may carry sulphites. A recipe that uses one of these names the allergen.

## 27. Tvaroh, cream and raw-milk dairy

- Risk groups avoid dairy products from unpasteurised milk, "podle SZPI" (listeria leaflet: https://www.szpi.gov.cz/soubor/informace-a-doporuceni-pro-spotrebitele-ve-vztahu-k-nebezpeci-vyskytu-listeria-monocytogenes-v-potravinach.aspx) and raw-milk soft cheese, "podle BfR" (https://www.bfr.bund.de/presseinformation/listeriose-selten-aber-gefaehrlich-fuer-alte-schwangere-und-personen-mit-verminderter-immunabwehr/). The label need not say the tvaroh is from raw milk. Smažený hermelín for risk groups only heated through, "podle britské NHS" (https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/keeping-well/foods-to-avoid/).
- Vyhláška 397/2016 Sb. (https://www.zakonyprolidi.cz/cs/2016-397): tvaroh by fat in dry matter (tučný ≥ 38 %, polotučný 15–25 %, nízkotučný ≤ 15 %, odtučněný ≤ 5 %); flavoured tvaroh is still tvaroh with the flavouring named. Smetana ≥ 10 % fat, ke šlehání ≥ 30 %, vysokotučná ≥ 35 %, kysaná ≥ 10 %.
- Opened tvaroh and cream: no Czech or EU number; follow the label (makers give "ihned" to 48 hours), like milk in topic 15. Baked goods with tvaroh filling, and opened tvaroh with no number on the label (2 days), follow topic 1: the site's own application.

## 28. Hot holding, trips, frying hygiene

- Hot food kept above 60 °C; cooked food at room temperature at most 2 hours, shorter in heat and sun (no number); take food for a trip cold from the fridge in a cool bag, "podle SZÚ" (https://szu.gov.cz/aktuality/proc-byva-v-lete-vice-pripadu-alimentarnich-onemocneni-a-jak-na-prevenci/).
- Raw-meat hygiene: wash hands and boards, never put fried food back on the raw-meat plate; breading leftovers that touched raw meat go away, "podle BfR" (topic 9 URL).
- Scalds: as topic 21; take off clothing soaked in hot liquid, leave what sticks (NHS).
